Thank You

Thank you for participating in this study.

This study is part of a programme of research examining how interacting with AI conversational systems shapes our social and cognitive experiences, compared to interacting with other humans. As AI tools become increasingly common in everyday life, it is important to understand whether and how they change the way we think, remember, and relate to others. Across different studies in this programme, we investigate questions such as: How do we respond when a conversational partner (human vs AI) provides agreeable, validating, or flattering responses? How well do we remember information depending on whether it was told to us by a person or by an AI? Your participation contributes to our understanding of these questions.

In this study, you were in the condition where you only interacted with a human. You viewed a series of scenarios and were asked to evaluate other people's actions. You were either in the condition where you were told that these scenarios were coming from a stranger or a friend.

To summarise the key points of this study

  • The purpose of this research is to examine how human cognitive and social processes are shaped by interactions with AI conversational systems, compared to human interlocutors.
  • Depending on the study, you may have viewed scenarios written by other humans or interacted with an agent.
  • Our main goal is to understand how human behaviour and the mind are influenced by the rapid adoption of AI tools.

Privacy and Confidentiality

Your identity in this study is deidentified; your ID number will not be included or in any other way associated with the data collected in the study. Your responses were securely transmitted and stored using DataPipe, a secure data-relay service built for behavioural research, and then archived on the Open Science Framework (OSF), a password-protected, access-controlled repository. All de-identified data will be retained for a minimum of seven (7) years. Furthermore, all de-identified data may be posted to an online repository to help facilitate the ongoing reproducibility effort within Psychological Science.

We ask that you do not discuss your experiences with any other individuals who potentially could be in the study. If people participate in the study knowing about our specific predictions, it could influence their responses, and the resulting data would not be usable.

We appreciate your participation, and hope that this has been an interesting experience for you.
